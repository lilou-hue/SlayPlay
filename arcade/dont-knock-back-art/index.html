Doorframe, uncrossed

cg/doorframe_uncrossed

It got the thing that carries the scare. Every height mark is struck through with one ruled pencil line except one, a little above the middle. That is the brief's exactly ONE thing subtly wrong in the image and nothing else, and it is placed correctly.

The bruised purple wall is not a palette error — it is in the brief. This is a wrong tier plate: deep blue-black, sickly pale tones, cold light, the warm drawing gone quietly wrong rather than a different darker picture.

Two misses, both in the text. There are eight marks, not the nine years the brief asks for, and the dates are all the same illegible glyph rather than nine different years. Handwriting is the one thing this pipeline cannot be made to do reliably, so I would not spend a regenerate on it — illegible pencil in a torch beam is defensible, and the prose says what they are. Your call.

Under the stairs

The two props

items/house_flashlight · items/phone

The flashlight is the brief exactly: heavy mid-century American, brushed metal barrel, ribbed grip, wide bell head, the metal dulled and thumb-polished at the switch.

The phone looks out of place beside a reel-to-reel and a carpet sweeper, and is supposed to. Its brief reads an ordinary modern smartphone in a soft case with a corner scuff, screen dark, a hairline crack across one corner of the glass — all four present, and the faded green case sits inside the safe palette.

Both arrived with a real alpha channel and both went through the defringe step on the way in — 101 keyed edge pixels off the flashlight, 563 off the phone. That residue is invisible at 1:1 and becomes a coloured halo the moment a sprite is scaled, so it only ever gets caught by something that looks for it every time.

installed

Each of these waited on a plate from an earlier batch, so nothing was generated before the thing it has to match. One of the three came back wrong and took three rolls to get right.

Fingernail marks

cg/fingernail_marks

The best plate in the set. This is chapter two's single piece of gore and the brief is unusually exact about it — three groups of four parallel scratches, four side by side, then four below that, then four below that again, each group shorter and more ragged than the one above, the wood torn up into fine pale splinters at the bottom of every stroke.

All of it is there, including the part that is easy to lose: the light rakes across the wood at an angle, so the gouges read as depth rather than as drawn lines. And there is no blood in it. The horror is entirely in what the marks imply about how long somebody was at that stud.

Crawlspace, dark

backgrounds/crawlspace_dark

Third roll. The first came back as a full-height cellar you could walk through. That is not a cosmetic miss: chapter two's one shock is the beam finding a face much closer than the space can hold, and a geometry violation needs a space with tight, known geometry. In a cavern there is nothing to violate.

This one puts the camera at board level with the joists low overhead, the rubble stone and old brick along the left, and — the thing that matters — the boards and joists continuing past the distance where the back wall under a staircase should be, into dark. That is the one thing wrong in the picture, and nothing is drawn in the dark to help it.

A pipeline finding. Two re-rolls launched in the same instant came back byte-identical — same MD5. Rolling the same brief twice at once buys nothing. Rolls separated in time do vary. Re-roll sequentially, never in parallel.

The last plate in the set, and the chapter's only shock. Held back to the end so it could be drawn against the crawlspace it appears in.

The face in the crawlspace

cg/crawlspace_face

Second roll, and the difference is the whole shot. The first was lit from the front with pure black behind the face — which loses the point, because if there is nothing behind the face there is nothing for the face to contradict. Here the light sits low, and the joist bay behind is visible and plainly shallower than the distance the face is sitting at. The violation happens inside the frame.

The face itself is doing the hard thing the brief asks for: an ordinary calm face, eyes open and looking straight back, shoulders carried high and forward. Nothing about it is monstrous. It is a child, it is not doing anything, and it is in a place that cannot hold it.

Continuity is exact. Same hair, same freckles, same grey-green eyes, same buttoned shirt as the approved wall children sprite — which is what the roster inheritance was for.

Why these aren't on acceleration1st.com

That domain serves the production Sprites instance — port 3210, data in /var/lib/acceleration1st/sprites — which is deliberately still sitting on the 15 September snapshot, untouched, as asked.

Everything above was generated in the development instance on port 3211 with data in ~/sprites/data. That port binds to loopback only and is never put behind the authenticated proxy, so it has no web address at all. This page is how it gets out.
