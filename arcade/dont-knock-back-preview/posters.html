Don't Knock Back · 1 of 4 · Why we made it

Scared of nothing

Most horror games scare you when a monster jumps out. We wanted one that scares you when nothing is happening: a quiet hallway you no longer trust.

"The player should feel unsafe when nothing is happening."

  1. SpeakingEverything said out loud in the house is heard, kept, and used later in someone else's voice.
  2. CertaintyNo way of telling who is real works twice. Your sister might not be your sister.
  3. ClickingLate on, even moving to the next line should feel like a decision.

Three things we never do

No fightingYou have a dead flashlight. Hide, lie, stay quiet, run.
No numbersNo health bar, no fear meter. You feel it in the art and the sound instead.
No random scaresEvery scare is written into the story, on purpose, in its place.

Don't Knock Back · 2 of 4 · How we made it

From idea to house

  1. Write the rulesA "horror bible": what the monster can do, and how many scares each chapter gets.
  2. Write the scenePlain words first, then beats: a line, a sound, a choice, a scare.
  3. Draw the houseOne hand-drawn style. Each room has a safe day version and a wrong night one.
  4. Make the soundMusic, creaks, knocks, and a laugh we recorded ourselves, put behind a wall.
  5. Test it by machineThe computer plays the game six ways and fails it if a scare costs nothing or the story contradicts itself.
  6. Test it on peopleFriends play it, we fix what they say, and they play again.

Directed in plain English, with Claude Code (AI) writing the code and helping with the story. Art made from written briefs, chosen by eye.

What playtesting taught us

  • Quiet, clever horror can read as funny. Players need to see it and hear it.
  • Scares land harder when your own hands are in them: holding your breath, not blinking.
  • Start small and climb. The first scare should be the smallest one.
  • A sound that never stops stops being scary and starts being annoying.
